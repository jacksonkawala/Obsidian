# Effective Stress

Effective stress is the stress carried by the soil skeleton. For saturated soil, rising pore pressure reduces effective stress and therefore reduces available shear strength.

In CSSM notation, mean effective stress $p'$ is the stress coordinate used with void ratio to define the [[Critical State Line]] and the [[State Parameter]]:

$$
\psi = e - e_c(p')
$$

This is why water management matters directly to liquefaction risk. If pore pressure rises while the applied shear stress remains broadly constant, the soil can move toward a constant-shear, decreasing-mean-stress path. Jefferies and Been treat this path as especially dangerous because it may give little warning before static liquefaction.

## TSF Relevance

For [[Nataka TSF]], effective stress links operational water levels, seepage, freeboard, and liquefaction susceptibility. A rising pond or phreatic surface can reduce $p'$ in loose tailings or embankment zones, increasing $\psi$ and moving the material closer to strain-softening behaviour.
