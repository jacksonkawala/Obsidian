# Nerlerk

Nerlerk B-67 was a Canadian Beaufort Sea artificial island/berm case history used by Jefferies and Been to illustrate static liquefaction in hydraulic fill and the importance of soil variability.

The berm used hydraulically placed sand and suffered six large mass failures, with the 1983 construction still roughly 10 m below the design level. The case became important because CPT data seemed to indicate denser material than the observed failures implied. Later interpretation showed that stress-level effects, shear modulus, and connected loose zones were central to resolving the apparent contradiction.

## CSSM Lessons

- Hydraulic fills can contain connected loose zones even when the average CPT profile looks adequate.
- Design should use a characteristic [[State Parameter]] value, not the mean.
- CPT inversion needs soil-specific properties and stiffness, not only tip resistance.
- Nerlerk is a bridge case between [[Chapter 1 — Introduction]], [[Chapter 4 — Determining State Parameter In-Situ]], [[Chapter 5 — Soil Variability and Characteristic States]], and [[Chapter 6 — Static Liquefaction and Post-Liquefaction Strength]].
